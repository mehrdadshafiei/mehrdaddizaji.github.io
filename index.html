About me

Mehrdad Shafiei Dizaji, Ph.D.

Structural Engineering · AI · NDE · Digital Twins

I am a civil and structural engineering researcher developing data-driven and physics-informed methods for structural health monitoring, nondestructive evaluation, infrastructure inspection, digital twins, and intelligent sensing.

My work connects structural mechanics, computer vision, deep learning, finite element modeling, and sensor data to create practical tools for infrastructure assessment, damage detection, and structural decision-making.

VDOT / VTRC · University of Virginia · Final report · May 2020

Load Rating Strategies for Bridges With Limited or Missing As-Built Information

Assessing bridge load-carrying capacity when drawings and structural details are incomplete. This collaborative project combined field measurements, finite element model updating, and vibration-based identification to estimate unknown structural properties and support analytical load ratings.

Bridge load ratingFull-scale field testingFinite element model updatingStructural identification
4 study bridgesTwo slab bridges and two T-beam bridges in Virginia
My roleGraduate Research Assistant, UVA · Report co-author
Engineering applicationMeasurement-informed assessment for bridges with limited records

Project findings

Across the four study bridges, ratings from finite element model updating were equal to or up to 17% lower than conventional calculations. The simplified vibration-based method produced differences from −16% to +16%. The study also demonstrated the feasibility of using a limited number of sensors for these methods.

Source: Harris, Ozbulut, Bagheri, Shafiei Dizaji, Ndong & Alipour, Load Rating Strategies for Bridges With Limited or Missing As-Built Information, FHWA/VTRC 20-R27 (May 2020). Figures reproduced from the report; © 2020 Commonwealth of Virginia.

Evaluation of Mecklenburg Bridge Following Repair Using 3D Digital Image Correlation

A field investigation of a repaired steel girder on the Mecklenburg Bridge, following earlier web buckling and crippling. Stereo-camera 3D digital image correlation captured full-field deformation during the lowering of the beam onto its bearing, supported by handheld laser scanning and conventional strain gauges.

Steel girder repair3D digital image correlationLaser scanningFull-field diagnostics
Mecklenburg County, VirginiaNorthbound I-85 over U.S. Route 1
My roleFirst author · Collaborative UVA / VTRC investigation
Engineering objectiveEvaluate repaired beam-end behavior during load transfer

Project findings

The monitored beam end showed no evidence of renewed local buckling following the lowering sequence. Full-field measurements and scan comparisons supported evaluation of the repaired region. The 3D-DIC results also enabled back-calculation of dead-load sharing, with conclusions limited to the single beam location studied.

Hampton Roads, Virginia · Full-scale field load testing

Evaluation of Five Spans of the Hampton Roads Bridge Tunnel

A field evaluation of five prestressed concrete girder spans using digital image correlation (DIC) and a cable-suspended reference (CSR) system. The testing addressed selected spans that did not pass load rating, with six VDOT tandem dual dump trucks filled with gravel providing controlled loading.

Work took place approximately one mile offshore, where an open-water setting offered no land-based fixed reference. The team installed instrumentation during the day and tested at night to minimize traffic disruption. Modified GoPro cameras mounted at pier caps observed midspan targets for DIC displacement measurements.

Designing High-Precision Fast Nonlinear Dam Neuro-Modelers and Comparison with Finite-Element Analysis

Abdolreza Joghataie & Mehrdad Shafiei Dizaji · Journal of Engineering Mechanics, 139(10), 1311–1324 · 2013

How can a neural network learn a dam’s response to an earthquake? This research develops a dam-specific neuro-modeler trained on finite element simulations. A moving window of ground acceleration and feedback from the previous crest response allow a feed-forward network to represent nonlinear, history-dependent behavior. The paper investigates the Koyna, Pine Flat, and Sefid-Rud concrete gravity dams.

Course Syllabus · Fall 2019 · University of Virginia

CE 5340 · Advanced Topics in Structural Engineering

Matrix structural analysis through the direct stiffness method, virtual work, and computational implementation. Instructors: Jose P. Gomez, P.E., Ph.D., and Mehrdad S. Dizaji.

Matrix analysisDirect stiffness methodMATLAB & MathcadFinite elements
Explore syllabus highlights

Prerequisite: CE 3300 Structural Mechanics or equivalent.

Course focus: Relate external loads to member forces, deformations, and global displacements using equilibrium, force–deformation relationships, and compatibility. Formulate element stiffness matrices, assemble the global stiffness matrix, and solve structural systems with MATLAB, Mathcad, and Excel.

Topics: Statics and matrix algebra; virtual forces and displacements; force and displacement methods; planar trusses, beams, and frames; symmetry, support settlements, releases, and temperature effects. Time permitting: three-dimensional systems, nonlinear behavior, and an introduction to finite element analysis using ABAQUS.

Assessment: Homework 10%; Test 1 20%; Test 2 20%; Computer Project I 25%; Computer Project II 25%.

Recommended texts: Matrix Analysis of Structures (Kassimali); Matrix Structural Analysis (McGuire et al.); Matrix Analysis of Structures (Sennett).
