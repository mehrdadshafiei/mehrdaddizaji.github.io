About me

Mehrdad Shafiei Dizaji, Ph.D.

Structural Engineering · AI · NDE · Digital Twins

I am a civil and structural engineering researcher developing data-driven and physics-informed methods for structural health monitoring, nondestructive evaluation, infrastructure inspection, digital twins, and intelligent sensing.

My work connects structural mechanics, computer vision, deep learning, finite element modeling, and sensor data to create practical tools for infrastructure assessment, damage detection, and structural decision-making.

05

Bridge Load Rating with Incomplete As-Built Information

Developed finite-element-model-driven strategies combining field measurements, bridge testing, and sensor data for load rating.

BridgesFEMLoad RatingVDOT
FEMU-BASED BRIDGE LOAD RATING / INCOMPLETE AS-BUILT INFORMATION

Based on the supplied paper, “Automated Finite Element Model Updating-based Load Rating of Bridges with Incomplete As-Built Information by Integrating Numerical data with Sensor-Based measurement.” The study evaluated two concrete slab and two T-beam bridges, treating available details as unknown and using plans as a comparison baseline. The hybrid method combined local static and global vibration response. The moving truck, response curves, and updating progress are illustrative; this preview does not compute a bridge rating factor.

Video-Based Modal Frequency Estimation

Developed a hybrid-attention CNN–ConvLSTM model to estimate modal frequencies from structural vibration videos, transferring knowledge from simple beams to an independent turbine blade using limited data.

Modal AnalysisConvLSTMHybrid AttentionTransfer Learning

Dizaji, Mao and Haile (2023), “A hybrid-attention-ConvLSTM-based deep learning architecture to extract modal frequencies from limited data using transfer learning.” Frequency values reproduce the paper’s DLVM estimates in Tables 2–3. 07

SMA Recentering Dampers for Seismic Protection

Investigated a passive recentering damper combining NiTiHfPd shape memory alloy bars and friction springs, using material tests and nonlinear seismic analyses of six- and nine-story steel frames.

Shape Memory AlloysSeismic ProtectionPassive ControlRecentering
SMARD / ENERGY DISSIPATION AND RECENTERING
Cyclic shaking

Based on Farzad Shafiei Dizaji and Mehrdad Shafiei Dizaji, “A Novel Smart Memory Alloy Recentering Damper for Passive Protection of Structures Subjected to Seismic Excitations Using High Performance NiTiHfPd Material.” Material and spring tests informed the proposed device and numerical building studies. The paper calls for further experimental validation of the complete device. This animation illustrates the mechanism and residual-drift concept; it does not reproduce quantitative test or building-analysis results.

08

Hysteresis Identification with EPNN

Developed an Extended Preisach Neural Network that combines deteriorating-stop memory neurons and sigmoid neurons to identify nonlinear hysteresis, including asymmetric, non-congruent, and rate-dependent behavior.

HysteresisNeural NetworksSystem IdentificationSmart Materials

Farrokh, F. S. Dizaji and M. S. Dizaji (2022), “Hysteresis Identification Using Extended Preisach Neural Network,” Neural Processing Letters. The loop examples and animated signals are schematic, normalized illustrations of the capabilities discussed in the paper. This preview does not run a trained EPNN or reproduce experimental data.

Application Demo · Augmented Reality

Structural analysis brought into the real world

Explore a bridge model through a mobile augmented reality interface that brings structural analysis results into the surrounding physical space.

From numerical results to spatial understanding

This recording demonstrates my augmented reality structural visualization work. The mobile interface offers element, member, and system analysis views. In the bridge example, a three-dimensional model appears in the camera view, allowing the user to inspect it from different viewpoints.

The demonstration shows a finite element mesh, load and support indicators, and color-coded structural response contours. On-screen controls switch between response components, including strain and displacement, while node information connects the visualization to locations in the model. This approach helps make structural behavior easier to explore and explain in engineering education and design discussions.
