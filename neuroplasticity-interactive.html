WHAT MAKES THE MEDIUM USEFUL?

Wave propagation provides spatial coupling and a time delay. Plastic deformation supplies history dependence. Adjusting the base material adds learning variables alongside the synaptic weights.

The paper uses an undamped nonlinear dynamic finite-element model with von Mises plasticity and isotropic hardening. It studies a constructed input–target problem to demonstrate the concept.

Conceptual 200-neuro-cell extension requested for this demonstration. The paper grows a one-to-three-neuron network; this view instead assigns one neuro cell to every FE cell. The medium contains exactly one layer of connected 3D-looking elements; raised dots are neural markers, not a second FE layer. This is not a reproduction of the finite-element solver or the training algorithm. Wave fields, output curves, intermediate learning states, and the smooth λ progression are illustrative. The reported final scales (λ₁₀₀ = 1.5, λ₁₂₀ = 0.5) and error milestones come from the paper. No biological-brain model or real-time learning performance is claimed.
