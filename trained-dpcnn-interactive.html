WHAT CHANGED IN TRAINING?

Here: only the output readout coefficients are fitted by ridge regression. The 200-cell dynamics and local tanh feedback are fixed. This is an executable teaching model, not a reproduction of either paper’s training algorithm.

NEW INPUT, SAME PARAMETERS

The response changes because the input and dynamical states change. Learning resumes only when an optimizer is explicitly enabled.

Training: minimize Σ ||ŷ(t; θ) − y_target(t)||² + regularization, subject to dynamics.
Inference: fix θ = θ*; integrate M ü + C u̇ + f_int(u, internal state; θ*) = f_input + f_neural.

NONLINEAR SYSTEM IDENTIFICATION

Learn a time-dependent input–output relation from paired excitation and measured response. Test on held-out histories before using the learned model for prediction. This is the application illustrated by the live target-following task.

PROPOSED BRIDGE EXTENSION

Use measured loads and responses to train a bridge-specific forward response model. Damage inference needs a separate inverse formulation, temperature and support variables, multiple sensors, and identifiability checks. Speedup requires reduction or a separate surrogate.

What exactly is computed here, and what would full DPCNN training require?

The demo integrates 200 coupled scalar oscillators with nearest-neighbor coupling, damping, a cubic restoring force, and local tanh force feedback using a fixed 0.04 time step. This normalized lattice has no finite-element shape functions, stress integration, or constitutive plasticity. Elevated neuron markers show local deck connections; they do not represent an extra material layer. The bridge has two Warren side trusses, top cross-bracing and schematic support symbols, inspired by the user-provided reference image. Its 306.92 m label is taken from that image; no dimensional bridge model or truss/support stiffness is solved. Skin and plate tabs explain different architectures; both use this same reduced teaching solver.

The target is an independent nonlinear damped oscillator driven by the same input. A linear readout of 36 displacement, velocity and nonlinear-activation features plus a bias is fitted on 2,200 samples of one multisine history after a 600-step warm-up. Validation uses a different multisine history, resets the dynamical state, discards its own warm-up and never refits the coefficients. Reported error is RMS prediction error divided by RMS reference response, without mean subtraction. Training amplitude is 1.0; other amplitudes are unvalidated.

For the research model, define the excitation histories, target output and loss first. Optimize the permitted synaptic weights and material variables through sensitivity analysis or another appropriate constrained optimizer. Enforce physical bounds and dynamic stability; use held-out loads and initial states. Save learned parameters together with the constitutive law and state-initialization procedure. Frozen parameters do not imply frozen plastic strain or hardening state.
